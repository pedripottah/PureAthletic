# PureAthletic — Research Preparation Pack

**Status:** Operational guide for adult-only prototype sessions
**Last reviewed:** 24 August 2026

This document turns the Phase 0 research plan into a small, executable first
round. It is preparation guidance, not approval for junior recruitment or
production use.

The checklist below is not a participant information sheet, privacy notice, or
consent form. Prepare those separately for the selected jurisdiction and storage
process before recruitment.

## Round 1 — adult prototype research

Run five or more adult sessions using seeded or fictional profiles and the
local prototype. Aim for:

- 2–3 adult football players or coaches; and
- 2–3 parents or guardians.

Participants must be adults. They should be comfortable discussing how they
plan training, schedule football commitments, and decide when to rest. Do not
recruit anyone who would need to perform exercise or follow an app
recommendation during the session.

The minimum useful sample is five completed sessions, with at least one player
or coach and one parent or guardian. Record role mix rather than names.

## Round 2 — professional review

Arrange this separately from the user sessions:

- one qualified youth-sport practitioner; and
- one safeguarding or privacy reviewer where available.

Give reviewers a versioned copy of the materials listed in
`docs/safety-review-packet.md`, the local prototype, the scenario results, and
the relevant automated test output. Record approval, conditions, evidence
requests, or rejection for each review domain. A review of the product idea is
not approval of the exact prototype version.

## Session procedure

1. Explain that this is prototype research, not medical or performance advice.
2. Confirm consent before taking notes; use a session ID instead of a name.
3. Ask about a recent planning decision before showing the prototype.
4. Run the current-prototype tasks in `docs/research-plan.md` without coaching
   the participant; discuss deferred inputs only after the observed journey.
5. Record first action, hesitation, help requested, misunderstanding, and final
   outcome for each task.
6. Ask the short questions in `docs/research-session-form.md` and check whether
   the participant can explain what to do next and why.
7. End by explaining the prototype limitations, note access, and deletion date.

## Participant safeguards

- Collect only contact details needed for scheduling and broad participant
  category/device information needed to interpret results.
- Do not collect exact birth dates, medical histories, precise locations, team
  names, or identifiable performance data.
- Do not ask anyone to exercise, test pain, or follow a recommendation.
- If a participant volunteers sensitive information, do not probe for detail;
  note only the minimum safeguarding action required.
- Stop and escalate through the approved safeguarding process if someone
  reports harm, distress, or a current safety concern.
- Store notes privately under the session ID and set a deletion date before
  recruitment. Do not commit identifiable notes to the repository.

## Consent checklist

Before each session, confirm that the participant understands:

- participation is voluntary and can stop at any time;
- the session evaluates the prototype, not the participant;
- the prototype is not medical advice and must not be followed as a training
  instruction during research;
- only limited anonymised notes will be taken, access-limited, and deleted on
  the stated date;
- anonymised findings may lead to product changes; and
- questions or safeguarding concerns can be raised privately.

For any future child session, obtain jurisdiction-appropriate guardian consent
and child assent through an approved process before recruitment. This document
does not authorize child sessions.

## Session record and decision rule

Complete one private record per session using the session form. Add the
prototype version, date, device/browser, consent status, tasks attempted,
observations, safety misunderstandings, accessibility barriers, and one
backlog decision. Assign each finding:

- evidence strength: direct observation, repeated pattern, or hypothesis;
- risk: low, medium, high, or release-blocking; and
- disposition: change, investigate, accept, or defer.

Treat a safety misunderstanding as release-blocking until it is explained and
resolved or explicitly accepted by the qualified reviewer. Do not average away
one severe misunderstanding because other participants were successful.

## Minimum evidence to keep

For each session, retain only the completed session form, anonymised findings,
severity, evidence strength, and a concrete backlog decision. After the round,
summarise:

- at least five completed adult sessions across relevant roles;
- the top five usability or safety findings and their dispositions;
- any accessibility barriers;
- unresolved or release-blocking misunderstandings; and
- the practitioner/safeguarding review decision and conditions; and
- the decision to continue, narrow, investigate, or stop.
